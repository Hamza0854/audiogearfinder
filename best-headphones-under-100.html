Best Headphones Under $100

A complete guide to the best headphones in the $50–$100 range — for music, studio monitoring, gaming, and daily commuting.

The $50–$100 Sweet Spot for Headphones

The $50–$100 price range is one of the most competitive segments in headphones. This is where consumer comfort starts to meet professional-grade audio quality. You will find studio monitoring headphones used by working engineers, open-back designs beloved by audiophiles on a budget, closed-back commuter headphones with serious passive isolation, and gaming headsets with proper positional audio — all for under $100.

The Sony MDR-7506 and Sennheiser HD 280 Pro are two of the most widely used studio monitoring headphones in the world — both under $100. That says a lot about the quality available at this price.

Best Studio / Monitoring: Sony MDR-7506

The Sony MDR-7506 ($90) has been the standard closed-back studio headphone for decades. Used in broadcast studios, recording sessions, film sets, and live sound environments worldwide. The sound is detailed, forward, and accurate — slightly bright, which makes it easy to hear subtle audio problems. The build is sturdy with comfortable leatherette earcups, and it folds flat for portability. For home studio work, podcasting, or critical listening, it is hard to beat at this price.

Best for Isolation: Sennheiser HD 280 Pro

The Sennheiser HD 280 Pro ($99) provides up to 32dB of passive noise isolation — among the highest of any non-ANC headphone. If you need to hear your recording mix clearly in a loud environment or want to block out office noise without electronics, the HD 280 Pro is an excellent choice. Sound signature is flat and analytical, similar to the MDR-7506 but with a slightly warmer low end.

Best Open-Back Value: Philips SHP9500

The Philips SHP9500 ($75) is one of the most praised open-back headphones under $100, especially among gaming and music communities. Its open-back design creates a wide, spacious soundstage that helps with directional audio in games and gives music a more natural, room-like quality. The 50mm drivers are large and energetic. Velour earcups make it comfortable for long listening sessions. Note: as an open-back design, it leaks sound and lets in ambient noise.

Best for Gaming: HyperX Cloud II

The HyperX Cloud II ($99) is one of the most recommended gaming headsets at this price. It includes virtual 7.1 surround sound processing via USB audio adapter, memory-foam cushions, and a durable steel headband. Sound quality is above average for gaming — clear highs for footsteps and detail, with enough bass for immersive explosions and soundtrack moments.

How to Choose the Right Pair Under $100

Match the Headphone to Your Environment

Open-back headphones (Philips SHP9500, Grado SR60x) sound more spacious but leak sound and let in noise. Use them at home in a quiet room. Closed-back headphones (MDR-7506, HD 280 Pro, HyperX Cloud II) are better for commuting, gaming, recording, or office use where you need some isolation from the outside world.

Wired vs Wireless at This Price

Under $100, wired headphones almost universally offer better sound quality than wireless ones at the same price point. Wireless headphones in this range sacrifice audio accuracy for the convenience of Bluetooth. If you need wireless for commuting or mobility, the Sony WH-CH720N is a good option. If sound quality is the primary goal, go wired.

IEMs vs Over-Ear Under $100

In-ear monitors (IEMs) like the Moondrop Aria can deliver audiophile-quality sound at budget prices — remarkable when the tuning is well-executed. They are compact and portable but depend heavily on getting a good ear tip fit. Over-ear headphones are usually more comfortable for extended listening but are less portable. The best choice comes down to lifestyle: portable listening favors IEMs, desk listening favors over-ear.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are headphones under $100 good?

Yes. Headphones under $100 can be excellent for everyday listening, music, gaming, and commuting. Models like the Sony MDR-7506 and Sennheiser HD 280 Pro are used by audio professionals worldwide.

Can I get noise-canceling headphones under $100?

Yes. Some headphones under $100 include active noise cancellation. Budget ANC works best on steady background noise like fans or road sound. It typically will not fully block voices or loud environments.

Are wireless headphones under $100 worth buying?

Yes, for most casual listeners. Wireless headphones under $100 offer Bluetooth convenience and decent battery life. For studio monitoring or critical listening, wired options in this range often deliver better accuracy.

Are studio headphones good for everyday listening?

Yes. Studio headphones like the Sony MDR-7506 are durable, accurate, and comfortable for long sessions. They sound more neutral than consumer headphones, which feels natural once you adjust.

What is the best over-ear headphone under $100?

For studio use: Sony MDR-7506 or Sennheiser HD 280 Pro. For gaming: HyperX Cloud II. For open-back listening: Philips SHP9500. Each excels in its intended use case.
